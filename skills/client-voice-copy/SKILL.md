# Write copy in the principal's voice

Draft public-facing copy in the principal's own words — through an interview method and their approval — before anything goes live. The "principal" is whoever the copy represents: your user, their client, a founder. Set up their voice profile once (register, forbidden words, honesty rules), then reuse it.

## Interview the principal section by section

- Ask the principal for the content of each section in their own words before you draft anything. One question per turn, with concrete phrasing such as "what does the product do in one sentence."

- Record their answers verbatim. Your draft may make small edits for clarity, but the words, claims, and framing stay theirs.

- If you have nothing of theirs to build a section from, go back and ask. Inventing the wording yourself is the failure this skill prevents.

## Draft from their words, not yours

- Build every draft from their verbatim answers, never from a feature list or trait list. A sentence that lists adjectives about them is a red flag. Rewrite it or drop it.

- Skip competitor prices, superlatives, and stock phrases like "unlock" or "leverage" unless they said them. Match their register: direct, plain, and specific beats polished and generic.

- Keep claims honest about what exists. If only one product is fully built and the rest are in development or proof of concept, the copy must say so.

## Propose before anything goes live

- Show them the exact text you intend to use and apply it only on their approval. This covers headlines, bios, About sections, project entries, and hero copy.

- When they approve with edits, apply the edits exactly, then confirm the final text before publishing.

- After publishing, verify it appears as written and tell them once it is live.

## Keep their writing rules

- Capture the principal's hard rules in their voice profile (example: "never use em dashes — use commas, periods, colons, or parentheses instead") and check every draft for violations before showing it.

- Match their voice, not your defaults. Short sentences and concrete nouns beat parallel triplets and abstract claims.

- Language and voice fixes are yours to make. Anything that adds, changes, or deletes what they are actually saying needs their approval first.

## Alternatives for better results

- When they push back on a draft, ask which of their own words you misused instead of rewriting cold. Their correction usually names the exact line to keep or cut.

- For long copy, draft one section, get approval, then move to the next. Approved sections become fixed anchors that keep later drafts in voice.

## Voice profile template

Keep one file per principal (e.g. `voice-profiles/<name>.md`):

```markdown
# Voice profile: <Name>

## Register
- Sentence length: short / medium / long
- Diction: plain / technical / lyrical
- Stance: direct / warm / contrarian

## Hard rules
- Never use em dashes.
- No superlatives unless the principal said them.

## Honesty rules
- Label what is built vs in development vs planned.
- No invented claims, metrics, or testimonials.

## Approval
- Principal approves every public-facing text before it goes live.
```
